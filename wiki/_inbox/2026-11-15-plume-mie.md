## Now
Rocket exhaust ice is lit with Mie optics, like the clouds' droplets. `python tools/make_mie_lut.py --ice` writes
assets/textures/plume_mie.rgba16f: ice spheres (Warren & Brandt index), 12 effective radii 0.05 x 60^(i/11) um
(0.05-3 um), effective variance 0.03 (ice frozen at once in one expansion), 2048 angles; its rgb keeps the SPECTRAL
scattering (dC/dOmega over the luminance-weighted mean C_sca), so sub-micron ice is blue (0.05 um: red 0.55x, blue 2.3x)
and 0.5-1 um ice shows corona colours 10-30 deg from the Sun; rows 12-23 hold each radius's sphere-mean colour (the
multiply scattered light's). The radius follows height (3 um below 15 km, 0.8 at 35, 0.25 at 60, 0.1 at 100, 0.06 above
150 km) and, inside a plume brick, the cube root of the local density (the ice grows on the exhaust's vapour). Both
plume renderers use it (the bricks and the Gaussians: cloud_march binding 29, header bit 30), and the CPU's old
Angstrom-exponent tint is skipped. Setting: launches.plume_mie (true). launches.plume_iridescence now varies the radius
in patches (x 0.55-1.8 at 1). Cost +0.13 ms at issue_20261115_165031's view; the composite stays at 128 registers.

## History
Plumes used 0.45 HG(g 0.8) + 0.55 isotropic and an Airy approximation for iridescence (0.25-0.75 um). With Mie by
height the staging cloud's 60-100 km ice is 0.1-0.25 um: bluer, broader forward glow, and the Airy model's pink patches
toward a twilight Sun mostly gone (only the dense core reaches the ~0.5 um that colours by angle).
