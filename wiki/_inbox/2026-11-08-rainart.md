## Now
The cloud march attenuates each lit sample's light by the air between the eye and that sample (`cv2AirOD`: Rayleigh
+ aerosol column on the straight line between the eye's and the sample's heights), and hands the sky pass an air
split distance where the air transmittance equals the samples' light-weighted mean (not their mean distance); the
exact path's attenuation at the split corrects the estimate. A ray that meets rain a few hundred metres out and cloud
tens of km out (the eye in or beside a rain curtain, looking at the horizon) thus gives the far cloud its haze and the
rain almost none. (The blocks the issue showed were a wildfire pall behind the rain taking the rain's split: see
2026-11-08-airsplit.md. With both, the reporter's horizon band std is 9.0 -> 2.8 levels.)

## History
2026-11-08, issue_20261108_172203 "Render artifacts within rain": jagged light/dark blocks along the horizon from
inside a rain curtain, gone on leaving it. Cause: one air split per pixel for near rain + far cloud. Tried and
reverted: the resolve's still history on the pixel's own distance (fixes the curtain view, 7x more dark unhazed far
shafts just outside it) and haze-space averaging of the distances (half the gain, +60% dark shafts). The rest was
smoke behind the rain (2026-11-08-airsplit.md).
