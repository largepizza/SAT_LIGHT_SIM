## Now
The weather cube's faces are 2048 x 2048 (~5 km a texel, the 8K source map's resolution), 9 mips. Only the low cloud's
placement read (`cv2WeatherSmooth`) uses the new finest level; every other read names the mip of the old 1024 cube
(`CV2_WX_LOD(l)` = l + 1), so types, rain, ground and the coarse lookups keep their scale. Level 0 computes coverage
and precipitation and takes type and ground from level 1 (precipitation from level 1 rained too widely: +4.5 ms of
cloud march in a storm view). A tropical cyclone's stamp (arms, filaments, eye) is averaged over
3 x 3 points of each texel it reaches. With storms stamped a face is re-baked once it is "Weather cube tick"
(`clouds_v2.weather_tick_s`, default 10 s, no UI) behind; faces out of view at a quarter of that rate; at 1x a face is
baked over 4 frames of rows, and in time warp the faces in view go by a quota per frame (ceil(faces in view x frame
step / tick)). Cost (RTX 3070 Ti, a hurricane from 950 km): a face ~0.7-1.3 ms; 1x a 0.13-0.3 ms slice on ~5% of
frames; 1 h/s ~5 ms a frame (four faces). Harness: `shaders reload weather=<spv>`, `state` -> `weather.cube.bake_ms`.

## History
Was 1024 faces re-baked every 45 s with storms stamped, the stamp sampled at each texel's centre: from orbit a
hurricane's arms stepped ~2 km at each re-bake and whole 10-km texels switched on and off (the user: "square cells
update and move in a blocky manner"). Frame-to-frame p99.9 change at 10x: 11-15 levels at each re-bake -> 3-4
uniform. The full computation at 2048 cost 1.85 ms a face (the convective-variance ring and the storm stamps
supersampled over every storm envelope); level 0 from level 1 and the stamp split brought it to ~0.7-1.3. A cubic
B-spline read of the cube was tried (+0.3 ms, smoother outlines, but it thins narrow arms) and left as a compile-time
option (`CV2_WX_BSPLINE`). Also fixed: with time paused, a cube baked on a jump's frame before the storms were uploaded
kept a hurricane without its arms until time ran.
