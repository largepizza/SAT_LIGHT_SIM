# 2026-11-11 perfrec: what the merged cloud march's slowdown is, and a faster return after a view change

- Attribution by setting knockouts (one live app, interleaved, the user's settings, 1600x900): at the ordinary views (orbit
  400 km, a 1.5-km cumulus field, a 4-km field) no merged feature's runtime cost is measurable -- the base band, the
  hurricane rotation / fill / eyewall / smoothing, the ice optics table, the storm moat frequency and the history time
  jump all sit inside the A-vs-A spread. The +2.5-3 ms at orbit against f1ce160 is the march's 255-register regime
  (docs/subsystems/clouds.md "The merged march's 255-register regime"), which no setting switches off. At the hurricane
  views the hurricane block itself is the cost at the fill view (-2.5 ms with it off) and a saving at the eye (off, the
  eye fills with cumulus to march: +10-17 ms).
- The "slow re-convergence after a view change" of the merged tree was a harness artefact: f1ce160's settle predates the
  2-s cap and ran up to 160 frames. On both trees a view change (a report, a bookmark, an observer jump) trips the cloud
  clipmap's motion guard, which held the clipmap off for 60 still frames (the march with no skips: the fill view 23-37 ms
  against 11 settled) and then re-baked it over ~70 frames: ~3 s to converge. The guard now returns after 16 still frames,
  and the bake is cheaper a frame (per-cell cyclone reach tests, exact; the footprint bracket's second read dropped where
  its two footprints are within 15% -- from orbit it read every point twice): the bake's pass 1.45 -> 0.84 ms a frame at
  orbit, 5.6 -> 5.2 at the fill view, 6.0 -> 5.7 over the eye.
- The two approximations proposed for a natural 128 (the beams' halos once per ray after the march; the hurricane fill's
  loop at the main sample only, the eye's bowl recomputed per sample) compile to 255 as well, as does every further
  removal on top of them (13 variants): not shipped. The register regime stays open.
