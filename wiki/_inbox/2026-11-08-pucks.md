## Now
A shallow cumulus cell (thinner than ~350 m in full, fading out by ~900 m; convective, not deep convection, a deck or a
storm mass) is shaped as a heap: its widest point is 0.12 of the way up (a flat base with a crisp edge) and the strength
needed to reach a height rises from there on a scale matched to the weak strengths such cells have (0.5 at the top
instead of 1.3), so its outline slopes in from the base all the way up and its height follows its strength. It is also
deeper by "Shallow cumulus depth" (`clouds_v2.cumulus_heap_depth`, default 0.8: x1.8). "Shallow cumulus heaps"
(`clouds_v2.cumulus_heaps`, default 1; 0 = the old flat-walled profile). Neither touches the base: it stays the
condensation level. At the Australian fair-weather field of the report the clouds' width / depth median goes from 3.8 to
2.8; cover from 25 km rises ~6% (more opaque rims), from orbit ~2%.

## History
2026-11-08 (issue_20261108_171510): shallow cells used the tall cells' profile, whose requirement is flat from the base to
the widest point at 0.55 of the cell, and their weak strength domed only the top fifth: kilometre-wide, ~250 m pucks
whose lower half was the 2D outline extruded straight up. Base-local fixes (a narrower stem, undamped lobes) did nothing
or brought back rounded "pie-crust" undersides; a lower widest point with the old dome scale left thin lenses.
