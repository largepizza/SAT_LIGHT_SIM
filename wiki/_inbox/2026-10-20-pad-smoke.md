## Now
**Launches raise ground clouds, and landings kick up dust or spray.** In the first seconds of a launch, while the
rocket is low over the pad, its exhaust and the deluge water's steam roll out along the ground. At a Starship pad the
cloud billows out all round, since its steel deflector sprays water up through the mount. At other pads most of it
shoots out of the flame trench behind the pad as a fan. The cloud rises because it is hot, and the wind carries it
for several minutes. Landing burns raise a cloud from the ground in their last seconds, and what it is depends on the
surface: steam and exhaust on a pad (a tower catch), tan dust on bare land, white spray at sea (around a droneship).
Everything is a pure function of sim time.

**The low trail and the ground clouds read as volumes.** Below a few kilometres the exhaust Gaussians share one
world-fixed billow field, so overlapping puffs form a single lumpy mass. Their faint tails are cut to give crisp,
cauliflower edges. Each puff is shaded by its own optical depth toward the Sun, and by the billow field's change
toward the Sun, so sunlit faces are bright and cores and far sides dark. Shaded sides take the blue sky's light. At
night the engines and pad floodlights light them orange.

**Plumes by day are about 13x brighter than before.** Their light was per steradian while the sky's is not (4 pi), so
a sunlit trail used to be as dim as the haze behind it. The twilight look is unchanged.

Harness: `launch next ... pad_dist_m=<m> [lead=<s>] [side=-1]` watches from the ground beside the pad.
`launch landing droneship=1` watches a booster landing at sea. `launch state` reports `ground_clouds`. The script is
`tools/harness/scripts/pad_smoke.satcmd`. The GPU cost is within noise.
