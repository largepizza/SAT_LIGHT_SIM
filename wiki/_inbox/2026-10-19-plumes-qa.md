## Now
A burn's exhaust trail is a polyline through its parcels: each piece spans the stretch between two neighbouring
parcels as they are now, so the trail bends and twists with the winds that carry it, and the newest piece runs from
the last parcel to the burning engines and grows with the burn - the trail leaves the stage continuously. Parcels are
emitted every 1 s (upper stages, deorbit burns) or 1-1.5 s (boosters). The exhaust is invisible at the nozzle and
condenses over 0.25 s low down to 1.5 s high up. The winds turn and change speed with height (strongly above ~35 km),
so high trails twist and kink over minutes. Each exhaust Gaussian carries billowy structure (the clouds' baked
Perlin-Worley noise, in the Gaussian's own size units, seeded per parcel; free where faint). Twilight plume colour: the
ozone's absorption is counted once along the Sun's path, and the ice's blue only where the sunlight keeps its blue -
no magenta band between the orange low trail and the white bell. Near the plume budget's cut, pieces fade by priority
instead of switching on and off.

Flames come out of each rocket model's engines: three emissive Gaussians behind the nozzle exits, sized from the exit
radius and lengthening as the air thins. Seen from afar they and the point (bloom, glare) carry the flame's magnitude;
once the flame is a few pixels wide it takes a surface brightness in the sky's own units, and the point's brightness
stops growing, so a close flame is a plume from the engines with a glare on its core, not a white sheet or a white disc
on the hull. A coasting stage's sunlit point disappears once its mesh is drawn.

Satellite and rocket meshes glare only where the reflection is a compact image of the Sun (roughness below ~0.1:
mirrors, quartz radiators, dielectric film, solar glass) - in the main view and the 3D viewer. Stainless steel and paint
no longer glare along their edges and panel seams.
