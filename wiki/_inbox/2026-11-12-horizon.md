## Now
The half-res cloud composite (`cloud_march.comp`) drops a resolved volumetric-cloud texel whose distance lies past the
texel's surface ONLY where that surface is a satellite or rocket mesh: there the resolve's history can hold the cloud
from behind a hull that moved in front of it. The scene depth buffer marks a mesh texel with `.g = 3`
(`scene_depth.comp`; elsewhere `.g` is the terrain's sun shadow, 0..1, or 2 = not computed). Terrain and the sea keep
their resolved cloud and haze: the resolved distance there is the opacity-weighted mean of the 3x3 new samples round the
pixel, which along a ridge against a hazy sky mixes in the sky's far dust and would call the dust in front of the ridge
"behind" it.

## History
2026-11-12, issue_20261112_215754 "Black line on sky terrain intersection" (1.9 km over the Zambian plateau, day): a
1-2 px dark band along every terrain silhouette against the sky, ~10 levels for the first ~60 frames after a history
reset and ~24 once the view settled (the reporter's frame). Attributed by knockout: `fog_layer` / `dust_amount 0`
removed it; the aerial-perspective and limb volumes, the sky TAA, the sparse grid, the full-res grid and the history
depth test did not; a composite without the drop removed it at every settle length. A first fix (fill a dropped
terrain texel from its same-surface neighbours) held only ~60 frames and painted the limb's haze onto a Starship.
