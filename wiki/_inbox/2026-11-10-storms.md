## Now
**Storm moats.** The clear moat round a storm is one ring, drawn twice: the weather bake lowers the low cloud's coverage
over it, and the march's mid layer (altocumulus) gives way over it. It runs from the edge of the storm's own cumulus
(1.15 x the tower radius + "Storm cumulus reach") out "Storm moat width (km)" (`clouds_v2.storm_moat_width_km`, 12), its
outer edge feathered over "Storm moat feathering" (`storm_moat_feather`, 0.6) of the width, round the share "Storm moat
frequency" (`storm_moat_frequency`, 0.5) of the storms (one draw per storm, from its dominant tower's seed), scaled by
"Storm clear moat" (`storm_moat`, 0 = no moat). Without a moat the mid layer gives way only over the towers themselves
(1.15 x their radius), so a storm stands in the deck around it.

**The cloud inside a storm.** A tropical storm's mass (the billowing towers of a hurricane's or tropical storm's dense
overcast) and the cumulus inside a storm object (within its towers' radius plus half "Storm cumulus reach") have their
own controls: "Storm tower height" (`storm_tower_height`, 0.85: the towers' height as a share of the cumulonimbus top;
0.85 is the mass as it was), "Storm fullness" (`storm_fullness`, 0.6: the floor between the towers rises and the
canyons between them fill), "Storm overhang" (`storm_overhang`, 0.4: heads standing out over narrower stems) and "Storm
puffiness" (`storm_puffiness`, 0.4: bigger lobes). "Storm cumulus top" (`cb_cumulus_top_km`) no longer cuts a tropical
storm's mass (it did through the low cloud's early-out bound) and shapes only the outer half of a storm object's reach
band, the cumulus round a storm. Settings > Weather > Storm objects, slots 568-574.

## History
- 2026-11-10 (issues 20261110_185127, 20261110_183113): every storm seen from orbit at a low Sun stood in a dark annulus
  with "Storm clear moat" at 0: the mid layer gave way out to ~0.6-1.0 of each tower's whole reach (its anvil's, ~50-80
  km), exposing the shadowed low field. The cumulus among a storm's towers took "Storm cumulus top" x (1 + its
  variation), and a tropical storm's mass was cut by the same "Storm cumulus top" bound: flat-walled drums with canyons
  between them (seen 6-9 km up inside the tropical storm Ancha).
