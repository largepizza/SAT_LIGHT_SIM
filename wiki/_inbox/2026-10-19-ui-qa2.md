## Now
The Info window offers one breakup action, **Explode**: a single click breaks the viewed satellite up with the NASA
standard breakup model's explosion law (always catastrophic, N(>Lc) = 6 Lc^-1.6) and starts the Kessler cascade from
it; the result shows as a toast. The harness keeps `kessler breakup <sat> kind=asat` (a 20-kg interceptor at 9 km/s
through the collision law) for testing.

The selection panel's second line says what the object is doing: a rocket shows "Rocket stage (<model>)", Starship's
ship "Starship ship, entry", a re-entry "Re-entry fireball", and a launch record its type and phase with its height
("<type> - raising to slot, 412/565 km", "parking orbit", "in the dispenser", "deorbiting"), refreshed every frame.

When a rocket hands over to what continues it (stack to upper stage at staging, upper stage to the ship's entry or
to its re-entry fireball, a train member to its roster slot, a retiree to its re-entry), the selection, the Info
window's 3D view and its pop-out follow it in the same frame: the view switches model with the same camera direction
and a distance scaled by the models' sizes; a fireball keeps the last model at its position. Go to keeps the object
where it was on screen through the handover, and staging is a seamless (0 m) handover: the upper stage starts from
the stack's point. Harness `state` -> `selection.panel_lines`, `selection.viewer_sat`.

## History
Until 2026-10-19 the Info window had Explode and ASAT strike, each needing a confirm click; the ASAT strike was the
same cascade from the player's side (the collision model with a small projectile: catastrophic below ~40 t, only
cratering a station) and the user asked for one button. The panel's second line printed the constellation's type,
and rockets and fireballs have none: a bare "?" under them. The 3D view kept the old model placed above the
observer after staging, an expendable stage's selection was dropped when its listing gave way to the fireball, and a
train member's selection ended at its arrival. The upper stage's track blended toward its orbit plane from 60 s
before staging on its own integration, ~4 km from the stack at separation, so Go to's camera was thrown off the
rocket at staging.
