## Now
Comets move on heliocentric conics (parabolas, hyperbolas, long ellipses alike, through a universal-variable Kepler
propagator from the perihelion state) in the planets' J2000 ecliptic frame, a pure function of sim time. The catalog
is the scripted Great Comet of 2037 plus ~6 minor comets a year hashed from (year, index): 60% long-period (q 0.35-2.75
AU, H 4.5-11), 40% Jupiter-family (q 0.9-2.3 AU, e 0.3-0.65, H 8-13), named like "C/2037 D1 (ATLAS)". The Great Comet
is a retrograde Kreutz-like sungrazer (q 0.0062 AU, i 142 deg) at perihelion 2037-02-17 16:49 UTC (behind the Sun,
mag ~-8, visible in daylight a degree or two from it the day before and after), a morning object brightening for weeks,
passing 560,000 km from the Earth over the northern hemisphere on 2037-03-17 03:30 UTC (new Moon; mag ~-6, a coma
~20 deg across, the tail across the sky), then an evening object fading over a month. Elements solved by
tools/comet_design.py against the sim's own Earth.
Brightness: m = H + 5 log Delta + 2.5 n log r_eff (r_eff saturates inside ~0.04 AU) with Marcus' forward-scattering
surge at high phase angle. The head (the ~1-arcmin inner coma) is a point through the point style (bloom, glare,
day suppression like satellites); the coma (1/rho), the ion tail (straight, along the solar wind as the comet sees
it, blue) and the dust tail (a syndyne/synchrone sheet: grains of radiation-pressure ratio beta released over the last
45 days, propagated under mu (1 - beta), striae along the synchrones) are grids of sky directions whose vertices carry
the physical surface brightness, drawn after the sky, at infinity (terrain, the Moon and opaque cloud hide them), gated
like the Milky Way against the sky's own surface brightness (city glow, twilight, daylight with the aureole, Moon) and
dimmed by extinction. Settings `comets.enabled`, `comets.minor`, `comets.tails`, `comets.tail_gain`,
`comets.display_gain` (no UI yet). Sky events kind "Comet" (the brightest under way or within a year; Watch lands at
its best moment at a dark place framing the tail). Comets are selectable (name, magnitude, distances, tail, coma,
perihelion). Harness `comets [state]|list|watch`.

## History
First tail cut put the perihelion dust in one log-spaced row (a jagged band): the emission-age grid now adds a dense
run round the perihelion's emission. The coma first saturated into a flat disc under 1 - exp(-x); the shoulder is
x / (1 + x).
