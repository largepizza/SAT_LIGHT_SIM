## Now
Squall lines, MCSs and strong multicells / supercells raise a haboob where their gust front crosses dusty ground (the
biome map's dust field): a billowing ochre wall 1.5-3 km tall a few to ~20 km ahead of the storm, with a forward-leaning
head and a thinner pall behind, riding with the storm. The wall gives back none of the sky's light and dims the Sun
spectrally, so the Sun behind it turns orange and the ground under it is shadowed. Setting "Haboobs (x)"
`clouds_v2.haboobs` 1 (Weather window > Controls). Harness `haboob`.

## History
First drawn as ordinary plume Gaussians: the plumes' "sky light scattered back" term made a thick dust wall the pale
grey of the sky whatever its colour. It now shares the wildfire smoke's range (own light only). Uncapped spectral
reddening then made it saturated orange; the reddening depth is capped.
