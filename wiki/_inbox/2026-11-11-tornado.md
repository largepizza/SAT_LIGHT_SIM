## Now
- A tornado's funnel, debris and wall cloud hide the air behind them. The sky pass adds the air in front of the
  composite up to a split distance; for plumes that are not smoke that split is two layers, the plumes and the cloud's
  mass each seen through the nearer one, at the plumes' near distance (a soft minimum of their closest approaches,
  3 km scale). A tornado's Gaussians always take part, even where a wildfire's glow reaches them.
- The cloud opacity profile the plume composite reads carries the depth the cloud's light comes from (luminance-weighted
  centroid, 12-bit log depth codes); the cloud's light is dimmed by the plumes in front of that depth.
- Cost: none measurable (tornado views 21.8-24.8 ms GPU, within +-0.15 ms, RTX 3070 Ti 1920x1009); the composite and the
  march stay at 128 registers.

## History
- Until 2026-11-11 the split sat at the cloud's mean distance whenever the plumes did not count as in front of the
  cloud's first contact, so from ~1 km AGL near a tornado the horizon air was drawn through the opaque plume in the
  outlines of near rain shafts and base lobes: black rectangles alternating with lit horizon, a rectangular notch in
  the funnel, a dark line under the storm (issues 20261111_171327, 20261111_171428).
