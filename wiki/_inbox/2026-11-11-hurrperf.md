# 2026-11-11 hurrperf: the hurricane cloud march (issue_20261111_164105 "Hurricane performance")

- What a hurricane view costs: the reporter's 74 ms cloud march was with the occupancy clipmap off (the clock at ~1 min/s and
  fast flight both switch it off). The light march is ~80% of the field reads there; 29% of the lit samples lie behind
  more than ~2.3 optical depths of their own cloud.
- The cloud march compiles to 128 registers again (it was 255 after the 2026-11-10 merges) through a register guard: two
  branches that never run (`CV2_REG_GUARD`, cloud_v2_march.comp), which steer NVIDIA's compiler; behaviour identical.
- Deep light (`clouds_v2.deep_light_t`, default 0.1, no UI): a lit sample whose transmittance inside its cloud is below
  0.1 borrows the previous lit sample's optical depth toward the light (+ its own extinction over the extra depth).
- Opacity stop (`clouds_v2.opacity_stop_t`, default 0 = off, no UI): the ray ends at that transmittance and its last sample
  takes the rest; 0.05 saves ~3-4 ms more over a hurricane, but adds bright spots on cloud edges (above the noise floor).
- Numbers (RTX 3070 Ti, 1600x900, cloud bucket): eye from 24 km still 67.4 -> 51.9 ms; the reporter's trail at 1 min/s
  62.8-65.4 -> 48.2 ms (GPU p90 108-113 -> 81); eye from 45 km 54-57 -> 47; under a hurricane 40-43 -> 38; orbit 400 km
  19.4 -> 18.3; cumulus 10.6 -> 10.3. Image at the A-vs-A noise floor at all three hurricane views.
