## Now
**The eye adapts to rocket light at night.** At night every engine light (burning engines, a ship's entry glow,
pad floodlights) is raised by the moonlight's own lift, which is right for an eye adapted to moonlight. Close to a
rocket the eye stands in far more light than that: 300 m from a Super Heavy it is about 260 full Moons. The lights'
strength is scaled by (1 + R / 2)^-0.75, where R is the illuminance at the eye from all the lights, in full Moons; their
reach shrinks with the square root. It applies only at night (blended by the Sun's height) and eases in about 0.3 s
toward darker, about 2 s back. Measured: x0.04 at 310 m, x0.11 at 1.5 km, about x0.5 from 2.5 km of a lift-off,
about 1 from 10 km or orbit. The flame itself, its point and the nozzle glow are not scaled.

**Lit exhaust.** Each exhaust Gaussian's light from an engine is averaged over its depth along the line of sight
(irradiance I / (r^2 + its variance along the ray + r0^2)), and the plumes are put in front of or behind the clouds by
the distance their light comes from, not their optical depth.

**Rocket and fireball labels.** Before lift-off a rocket reads "on the pad, T-9 s". A returning Starship ship after
its entry reads "belly-flop descent", "flip and landing burn", "caught by the tower" or "splashed down". Speeds are
over the ground below 80 km and inertial above 150 km (blended between), in the label and the Info window's STATUS.

**Info window.** For a burning rocket stage the MAGNITUDE tile and BRIGHTNESS rows give the flame's magnitude as the
ground observer sees it (marked "(flame)" above the air). A roster satellite that has been destroyed or re-entered
reads "Destroyed" / "Re-entered", with Explode, Go to and Track disabled.

**Go to again.** The "Go to <object> again" chip after leaving Go to disappears when sim time jumps by more than 10
minutes (its label described the object as it was when left).

**Mission facts.** A roster satellite whose replacement would launch after the Sun's Fury reads "Replacement: none
scheduled (launches stand down at the Sun's Fury)".

**Harness.** `kessler breakup sel` with nothing selected is an error (it broke up satellite 0). `launch state` ->
`engine_light_adapt`.

## History
- 2026-10-20 (QA sweep): following a Starship 310 m away at T+49 s at night, the whole view was a saturated
  orange-white haze and the selection read magnitude -18.5. The haze was the exhaust lit by the flame at the night lift
  (hundreds of times past white); the magnitude is the flame's physical brightness at that range, as seen from the camera.
  Before this, a pre-launch rocket read "coasting, T+-4 s, 0.41 km/s" (the Earth's rotation), a ship being caught read
  "deorbit burn", the Info window read "Dark" for a burning stage at night and "Operational" for an exploded satellite.
