## Now
Reflected clouds (rendering/sea.md, "Reflected clouds"): the on-screen cloud targets are no longer read at the
reflected DIRECTION's projection. The reflected ray is followed from the sea point: it is sampled where it crosses
0.3, 0.9, 1.6, 2.6 and 5 km altitude, each sample is projected into the camera and compared with the cloud target's
distance there (alpha, km); the first sample within -max(200 m, 2%) .. +max(2.5 km, 30%) of the visible cloud's
distance is the hit, refined by one bisection, and every cloud read (the five vertical taps, the per-texel opacity
weights, the emissive tests) happens at the hit's UV. No hit while on screen = clear sky (the clear march). A ray that
leaves the frame before a hit fades to the off-screen fallback (30% past the edge, by where it left). The terrain
occlusion of the reflection still reads the mirrored direction. Clouds the camera cannot see (behind nearer clouds,
off screen) are not reflected. Cost: sky pass +0.23 ms, GPU total +0.09 ms at 1920x1009 on an RTX 3070 Ti.

## History
Until 2026-11-12 the reflection read the cloud targets at the mirrored direction, i.e. assumed every cloud at
infinity. Near cumulus (1-2 km up, 5-60 km out) then reflected at the wrong place: bright blobs in a band just below
the horizon under no cloud, sliding with camera motion, and the near sea mirrored whatever cloud lay at the top of
the frame (issues 20261112_220250 / _220359).
