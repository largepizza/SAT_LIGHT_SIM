## Now
The multiple-scattering diffuse field needs the optical depth on the cloud's far side, away from the light (tauB). Two
field reads along the light's line, 0-300 m and 300-1200 m out (`ms_back_m`), estimate it; under a high Sun that line is
near vertical and passes under a shallow cumulus' base, so the reads are floored by the column's own path to its base
(the extinction x the height above the base / the Sun's sine, capped at `ms_back_m`), faded out for Sun elevations below
~17-44 deg where the line runs along the layer. Shallow cumulus seen from above are white-topped; their walls are no
brighter than their tops.

## History
2026-11-08 (issue_20261108_172314, issue_20261108_171510): without the floor the reads saw the clear air under 240-m cells
(tauB 0.6-2.7 against ~10), so their sunlit tops were lit like wisps (grey-blue, translucent) while the wall under the top
rim was brighter ("dark, then suddenly brighten near the ground": the rise down a side measured +25..+98%, now 0%). Orbit
and low-Sun views unchanged. The "extruded" lower wall of these shallow cells (a flat requirement below the widest point)
was investigated and left: every base-local change tried (stems, a lower widest point, undamped lobes) either did nothing
visible or brought back the rounded "pie-crust" undersides.
