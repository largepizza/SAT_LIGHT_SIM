## Now
Every row of the Missions window has ONE button, "Watch", that lands on the event already happening at 1x with the
subject in frame and, when it moves, selected and tracked by the camera lock. A launch: 8 s after lift-off, on the
ground 5 km from the pad, FOV 40. The pinned twilight launch ("jellyfish", now the Sun 5-10 deg below the pad, was
5-16): the first moment after T+150 s its upper stage leaves the Earth's shadow + 40 s, from 40 km, FOV 60. The Sun's
Fury: the storm night (aurora up). Ongoing: ride along with the object (Go to); retirees: one burning up (mid-glow,
45 s after its glow starts, under the clearer side's sky, FOV 35). Kessler tab breakups: 3 s after, 80 km beside and
25 km below the orbit, a piece tracked. Settings > Launches "Watch the next launch / a twilight launch / a Starship
catch (in its landing burn) / a retired satellite re-enter" and "Watch the next re-entry" do the same. The tutorial's
Missions cards point at the single Watch.

## History
The user (2026-10-24): "When a use wants to watch a lanch, they should just get one button that takes them to a rocket
launching ... These '1 minute before touchdown' type event calls do a real diservice as they often confuse playtesters
into teleporting to a place with nothing going on (yet)." The window had Go to (45 s before lift-off beside the pad),
Watch (the time jump only), Select, Next > (the next milestone) and two Sun's Fury buttons (2 min before the arrival,
the storm night). Twilight launches at a -11..-13 deg Sun showed a black sky: the exhaust bells stay in the Earth's
shadow. A breakup's recorded place was ~8100 km from its pieces for one engine collision (open).
