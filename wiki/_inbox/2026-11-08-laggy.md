## Now
A STILL view marches its clouds on a 3x3 sparse grid (one ray per 3x3 block a frame) instead of 2x2: once nothing has
moved for 64 frames (no turn, step, zoom or time warp; 1x time counts as still), below 30 km (`full_rate_above_km`), with
a valid history. The first moving frame is 2x2 again, so motion looks as before. The resolve gives the pixels without a
ray 3 / (K^2 - 1) of their usual weight in this mode, so each pixel's own rays keep the 2x2 grid's share against the
interpolated estimate and the settled image stays as sharp. Setting `clouds_v2.sparse_block_still` (3 = on, the default;
2 = off; no slider); `state` -> `clouds_v2.sparse_block_now`.
Measured (RTX 3070 Ti, 1920x1009, cloud march still): a sunset anvil deck overhead 31.4 -> 21.4 ms and 30.2 -> 23.7, an ITCZ
tower 18.7 -> 13.2, cumulus 11-12.5 -> 9.2-9.8; orbit unchanged; settled images within the run-to-run floor.

An issue report's `frame_ms` (the last 240 frames) counts only interactive frames: not the HQ photo's offscreen frames,
nor the frame after the photo.

What a big storm deck costs (profiling): its cloud, not its weather. At the report's anvil the storm objects are ~16 of
31 ms of cloud march, half of it the light march (Sun, Reflect beams, sky probe); the storm search loops ~2-4 ms; the
weather's CPU work ~0.6 ms a frame at 1x and none paused.
